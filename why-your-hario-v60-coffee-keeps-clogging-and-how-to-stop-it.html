Why Your Hario V60 Coffee Keeps Clogging and How to Stop It

2026-10-12

A clogging V60 almost always comes down to one thing: too many fine particles (fines) sealing the paper filter so water can't drain. Fix it by coarsening your grind, pouring more gently, and skipping the aggressive stir — in that order. Most people solve it in a single brew by going one or two clicks coarser.

Here's why it happens and exactly how to stop it.

What's actually clogging (it's not the coffee "being slow")

The V60's cone shape and large single hole mean it relies entirely on the paper filter and the coffee bed to control flow. When drawdown stalls, water is pooling on top long after you've stopped pouring — a brew that should finish around 2:30–3:00 for 15–20g suddenly takes 4, 5, even 6 minutes.

Three culprits, in order of likelihood:

  1. Too many fines. Fine particles migrate down and pack against the paper, forming a near-watertight seal. This is the #1 cause.
  2. Over-agitation. Hard pours and vigorous stirring shear the grounds, creating more fines and driving them into the filter.
  3. Filter paper clinging to the wall / double-walling. Trapped air or a stuck seam redirects water and chokes flow.

Bitter, hollow, over-extracted coffee usually rides along with clogging — because water sits in contact with grounds far too long.

Fix #1: Grind coarser (start here)

This resolves the majority of clogs. If your V60 tastes bitter and drains slowly, you are almost certainly grinding too fine.

  • Go one to two clicks/settings coarser and brew again. Aim for a texture like coarse table salt, noticeably coarser than espresso.
  • Target total brew time of roughly 2:30–3:15 for a 15–25g dose. If you're well over that, keep coarsening.

The deeper issue is often the grinder itself. Inexpensive blade grinders and worn burrs produce a wide, uneven particle distribution — a pile of boulders and a cloud of dust. The dust is what clogs you. A decent burr grinder with consistent particle size is the single biggest upgrade for pour-over.

If you already own a burr grinder, pull the burrs and clean them. Built-up oils and stale grounds degrade consistency over time.

Fix #2: Pour gently and control agitation

Even with a good grind, a violent pour creates and mobilizes fines. Technique matters.

  • Bloom with about 2x the coffee weight in water (e.g., 40g water for 20g coffee), for 30–45 seconds. This lets CO₂ escape so it doesn't disrupt later pours.
  • Pour in slow, controlled circles from a low height. Keep the stream thin. Avoid blasting the center or digging a crater down to the paper.
  • Don't drill the bed. Skip hard stirs. If you want to even out the grounds, a gentle swirl of the whole dripper at the end is far safer than a spoon.

A gooseneck kettle makes controlled pouring dramatically easier — the narrow spout gives you a slow, precise stream that a standard kettle simply can't match.

Fix #3: Rule out the filter and dripper

Sometimes it's not your coffee at all.

Rinse the paper first

Always rinse the filter with hot water before adding grounds. This washes away papery taste and seats the paper against the cone so it doesn't flap or trap air mid-brew. Dump the rinse water before you start.

Check for a double wall

Hario filters occasionally stick together. Gently rub the top edge and make sure you've got a single layer. Two layers of paper roughly double the resistance and will stall your drawdown.

Use the right filter

V60 papers are engineered for fast flow. Generic cone filters are often thicker and slower. If you switched brands right before the clogging started, switch back and test.

Hario V60 paper filters

Match filter and size

An 02 dripper needs 02 filters. A filter that's too large folds over the single drain hole and restricts it.

A clog-proof recipe to reset your dial-in

Use this as a known-good baseline, then adjust one variable at a time:

  • Dose: 20g coffee, medium-coarse grind
  • Water: 320g total, at 94–96°C (just off the boil)
  • Bloom: 40g water, swirl gently, wait 40s
  • Pour 1: up to 180g in slow circles, finish by ~1:15
  • Pour 2: remaining water to 320g by ~1:50
  • Gentle swirl, then let it draw down
  • Target finish: 2:45–3:15

If it finishes much faster than 2:45 and tastes sour, go a touch finer. If it stalls past 3:30 and tastes bitter, go coarser. Change grind before anything else — it has the biggest effect on flow.

Quick troubleshooting table

  • Draws down fine but tastes weak/sour → grind finer or pour slower.
  • Stalls every time, tastes bitter → grind coarser, this is classic clogging.
  • First pour drains fast, later pours stall → fines are migrating; coarsen and pour gentler.
  • Clogs only with one coffee → very fresh, very dark, or oily beans shed more fines and gas; coarsen slightly for those.

A note on beans

Oily dark roasts and very fresh coffee (within a few days of roast) both degas aggressively and tend to clog more. You don't need to avoid them — just grind a step coarser and give a slightly longer bloom. Light roasts are denser and usually want a finer grind and hotter water, so dial each coffee in on its own.

FAQ

Why does my V60 clog with fresh coffee but not older coffee?

Fresh coffee releases far more CO₂. That gas erupts during the bloom and early pours, churning the bed and pushing fines into the filter. Extend your bloom to 45 seconds and grind a touch coarser for beans within a week of their roast date.

Is a paper filter clog a sign my grinder is bad?

Often, yes. Blade grinders and worn or dirty burrs produce excessive fines — the exact particles that seal the paper. If coarsening the grind makes your coffee taste thin and sour before the clogging stops, your grinder can't produce a clean enough particle size, and that's the real bottleneck.

Should I stir my V60 to speed up drainage?

No — stirring usually makes clogging worse by generating more fines and driving them downward. If you want to promote even extraction, gently swirl the entire dripper after your final pour instead of stirring the bed with a spoon.
